# The All-BeES Portfolio — ₹1 Crore, 20-Year Horizon

*Built entirely from the Nippon India "BeES" exchange-traded fund family. Educational construction, not personalised investment advice. Verify every expense ratio and price on the AMC factsheet and NSE before you place a single order.*

---

## 1. The honest version of the goal

"Beat 99% of mutual funds over 20 years" is two very different bets depending on the peer set:

| Peer set | Realistic odds for this portfolio |
|---|---|
| Active **large-cap** funds (where most Indian retail rupees actually sit) | **Very high.** 76.3% of them lost to their benchmark over the 10 years to Dec 2025 — *before* counting the funds that were merged away. Add the regular-plan fee gap and a size tilt, and top-1% here is a structural near-certainty, not a forecast. |
| Active **flexi-cap / large-and-mid** funds | **High.** Same fee arithmetic, and this portfolio's mid-cap weight removes the main reason flexi-caps beat a plain Nifty tracker. |
| **The entire** mutual fund universe including small-cap and thematic funds | **Not certain, and nobody can make it certain.** The top 1% of a 20-year table is populated by concentrated small-cap funds that happened to run hot. You cannot buy that outcome in advance; you can only buy a high-probability seat in the top decile. |

**What this design actually promises:** a ~1.5% per year structural head start that compounds into roughly **₹2.4 crore of extra terminal wealth** on ₹1 crore over 20 years, with no manager risk, no style drift, and no fund-selection decision to get wrong.

That fee gap is the whole engine. Everything else is risk management.

**On "all BeES":** holding literally every BeES would mean paying three separate fees to own HDFC Bank four times over. The nine sector/thematic BeES overlap so heavily with the three broad ones that adding them raises cost and lowers diversification simultaneously. The portfolio below uses the whole family as a *menu* and takes eleven of them — the ones that add an exposure the others do not.

## 4. Why this beats the field — the arithmetic

Assume every asset delivers the same 12.5% gross. The only variable is what is skimmed off.

| | Blended fee | Net return | ₹1 Cr after 20 years |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| This portfolio | 0.32% | 12.18% | **₹9.96 Cr** |
| Direct-plan active fund | 0.90% | 11.60% | ₹8.99 Cr |
| Regular-plan active fund | 1.85% | 10.65% | **₹7.57 Cr** |

**₹2.39 crore** of difference, created by nothing but not paying it away. No forecast, no manager, no timing.

Now layer the two effects fees alone don't capture:

- **Size tilt.** 34% in Next-50 + Midcap-150 versus a large-cap fund's ~5%. If that adds just 0.75% a year, terminal value goes to **₹11.4 Cr**.
- **Rebalancing across uncorrelated assets.** Selling gold at highs to buy PSU banks at lows — mechanically, on a rule, without an opinion — has historically added 0.3–0.8% a year to multi-asset portfolios. It also cuts drawdown, which is what actually keeps you invested for 20 years.

Scenario range on ₹1 crore, net of costs:

| Scenario | Net CAGR | 20-year value |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pessimistic (India de-rates, flat metals) | 9.0% | ₹5.6 Cr |
| Conservative | 11.0% | ₹8.1 Cr |
| **Base case** | **12.2%** | **₹9.96 Cr** |
| Size + rebalancing premium delivers | 13.5% | ₹12.6 Cr |
| Optimistic | 15.0% | ₹16.4 Cr |

*Optional accelerant:* adding ₹1,00,000/month on top, indexed at 8% a year, roughly doubles the terminal figure in every scenario.

## 5. Execution — how to actually place ₹1 crore

**Do not deploy it in one day.** A single bad entry date costs more than a decade of expense-ratio savings.

### Deployment schedule

| Tranche | Timing | % of target | Amount |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | Day 1 | 40% | ₹40,00,000 |
| 2 | Month 2 | 20% | ₹20,00,000 |
| 3 | Month 4 | 20% | ₹20,00,000 |
| 4 | Month 6 | 20% | ₹20,00,000 |

Undeployed cash sits in LIQUIDBEES (over and above the permanent 7%).

**Accelerator rule:** if the Nifty 50 closes more than 12% below its 52-week high, pull the *next* tranche forward and deploy it immediately. Weakness is the schedule's friend, not its enemy.

### Order mechanics — these matter at this size

- **Limit orders only.** Never market-order an ETF. ₹20L of MID150BEES market-ordered into a thin book can cost 0.5% instantly.
- **Check iNAV first.** NSE publishes indicative NAV live. If the ask is more than ~0.5% over iNAV (2% for HNGSNGBEES), wait.
- **Avoid the first and last 15 minutes.** Spreads are widest at the open and close.
- **Split large legs across 2–4 days.** NIFTYBEES and BANKBEES will absorb ₹20L cleanly; MID150BEES, DIVOPPBEES, PSUBNKBEES and INFRABEES are thinner — work those orders.
- **Above ~₹25 lakh in one ETF, ask the AMC about direct creation-unit subscription.** You transact at NAV instead of paying the spread.
- Everything needs a demat account. Keep all eleven in one demat so the annual review is one screen.

## 6. The rulebook — this is what actually produces the result

The allocation is the easy part. These six rules are the part that fails in practice.

**Rule 1 — Rebalance on bands, not on feelings.** Review quarterly. Act only when a sleeve drifts more than **±25% relative** to its target (MID150BEES at 20% target → act outside 15%–25%). Plus one mandatory review in the first week of April.

**Rule 2 — Sell nothing inside 12 months.** Equity STCG is 20%; LTCG is 12.5%. Waiting three weeks to cross the anniversary is a guaranteed 7.5% return on the gain.

**Rule 3 — Rebalance with cash before you rebalance with sales.** LIQUIDBEES income, dividends and any fresh savings go to the most underweight sleeve first. Selling is the last resort, because selling is taxable.

**Rule 4 — Harvest ₹1.25 lakh of LTCG every single January–March.** Sell enough equity ETF to realise exactly ₹1.25 lakh of long-term gain, buy it straight back. The gain is tax-free, and your cost basis resets upward. Worth ~₹15,625/year in avoided future tax — around **₹8–10 lakh** across 20 years once compounded.

**Rule 5 — The crash protocol, written down now, executed then.** Nifty 50 down 25% from its high → move 3 of the 7 LIQUIDBEES points into MID150BEES and JUNIORBEES. Down 40% → deploy the rest, and sell GOLDBEES down to 7% to buy more. This is the single highest-value rule in the document, and the hardest to follow.

**Rule 6 — Cap the cyclicals, permanently.** PSUBNKBEES and INFRABEES are volatility harvesters, not convictions. If either doubles its weight, trim it back to target regardless of how good the story sounds.

**Optional glide path:** if the ₹1 crore is needed *at* year 20 rather than beyond it, from year 15 shift 3 percentage points a year from equity into GOLDBEES and LIQUIDBEES, so the outcome isn't hostage to one bad final year.

## 8. What would make this fail

An honest list, because a portfolio document without one is marketing.

1. **You sell in the drawdown.** 75% equity plus 15% metals can fall 40–45% peak-to-trough. This has happened repeatedly and will happen again inside 20 years. Every projection above assumes you did nothing. If you can't hold that, cut equity to 60% and accept a lower expected return — a portfolio you keep beats a better one you abandon.
2. **India's 20-year growth story disappoints.** No amount of low-cost structuring saves you from a flat market. This is the irreducible bet.
3. **Metals go nowhere for a decade.** 1980–2000 gold did roughly that. The 15% weight is sized so this is survivable, not fatal.
4. **Expense ratios drift up.** Recheck at every April review. If NIFTYBEES ever stops being among the cheapest, the thesis says move — the loyalty is to the cost, not to the brand.
5. **You rebalance too often.** Every trade is a spread plus a tax event. Bands, not tinkering.
6. **HNGSNGBEES premium trap.** Buying a suspended-creation ETF at a 6% premium is lighting 6% on fire. Check iNAV or skip it entirely.
7. **Concentration risk you didn't plan for.** ₹1 crore should not be everything. Keep 6–12 months of expenses and any 5-year goal money completely outside this portfolio, or Rule 5 becomes impossible to follow.
