The goal

What “beat 99%” can and cannot mean

The target only becomes answerable once you name the peer set. Against three different fields, the same portfolio has three very different sets of odds — and only honesty about that makes the rest of this document useful.

Peer setOdds for this portfolio
Active large-cap funds
where most Indian retail money sits		 Very high. 76.3% of them lost to their benchmark over the ten years to December 2025 — before counting the funds quietly merged away. Add the fee gap and a size tilt and top-percentile here is structural, not a prediction.
Flexi-cap and large-&-mid funds High. Same fee arithmetic, and this portfolio's mid-cap weight removes the main reason a flexi-cap beats a plain Nifty tracker in the first place.
The entire fund universe
including small-cap and thematic		 Not certain, and nobody can make it certain. The literal top 1% of a twenty-year table is filled by concentrated small-cap funds that ran hot. You cannot buy that in advance. You can buy a high-probability seat in the top decile.
The promise here isn't a return. It's a ~1.5% per year head start that nobody can take away — worth roughly ₹2.4 crore over twenty years, with no manager risk and no fund-selection decision left to get wrong.

Condition on HNGSNGBEES

It is the family's only real currency and geography diversifier, but the industry-wide US$7 billion overseas investment cap has repeatedly forced Nippon to suspend fresh creation — and a suspended ETF drifts to a premium over its indicative NAV. Buy only if the premium is under 2%. Otherwise route the 3% to GOLDBEES and move on. Paying a 6% premium for diversification is lighting 6% on fire.

The family

Which BeES earn a place, and which don't

Holding literally every BeES would mean paying three separate management fees to own HDFC Bank four times over. The nine sector and thematic products overlap so heavily with the three broad ones that adding them raises cost and reduces diversification at the same time. Accelerator rule

If the Nifty 50 closes more than 12% below its 52-week high, pull the next tranche forward and deploy it immediately. Weakness is the schedule's friend, not its enemy. Undeployed cash waits in LIQUIDBEES, over and above the permanent 7%.

Order mechanics — these matter at this size

  • →Limit orders only. ₹20 lakh of MID150BEES market-ordered into a thin book can cost half a percent instantly.
  • →Check iNAV first. NSE publishes it live. If the ask is more than ~0.5% over iNAV — 2% for HNGSNGBEES — wait.
  • →Skip the first and last fifteen minutes. Spreads are widest at the open and the close.
  • →Split the thin legs across two to four days. NIFTYBEES and BANKBEES absorb ₹20 lakh cleanly; DIVOPPBEES, PSUBNKBEES and INFRABEES need working.
  • →Above roughly ₹25 lakh in one ETF, ask the AMC about direct creation-unit subscription — you transact at NAV instead of paying the spread.
  • →Keep all eleven in one demat account, so the annual review is a single screen.

Discipline

The six rules that actually produce the result

The allocation is the easy part and takes an afternoon. These are the part that fails in practice, so they are written down now — while nothing is happening — to be executed later, when something is.

  1. Rebalance on bands, not on feelings

    Review quarterly, act only when a sleeve drifts more than ±25% relative to target — MID150BEES at a 20% target means acting outside 15–25%. Plus one mandatory review in the first week of April.

  2. Sell nothing inside twelve months

    Equity short-term gains are taxed at 20%, long-term at 12.5%. Waiting three weeks to cross the anniversary is a guaranteed 7.5% return on the gain.

  3. Rebalance with cash before you rebalance with sales

    LIQUIDBEES income, dividends and fresh savings go to the most underweight sleeve first. Selling is the last resort, because selling is a taxable event.

  4. Harvest ₹1.25 lakh of long-term gains every January–March

    Sell exactly enough equity ETF to realise ₹1.25 lakh of long-term gain, then buy it straight back. The gain is untaxed and the cost basis resets upward. Roughly ₹15,625 a year in avoided future tax — about ₹8–10 lakh across twenty years, once compounded.

  5. The crash protocol, written now, executed then

    Nifty 50 down 25% from its high: move three of the seven LIQUIDBEES points into MID150BEES and JUNIORBEES. Down 40%: deploy the rest, and sell GOLDBEES down to 7% to buy more equity. The highest-value rule here, and the hardest to follow.

  6. Cap the cyclicals, permanently

    PSUBNKBEES and INFRABEES are volatility harvesters, not convictions. If either doubles its weight, trim to target regardless of how good the story has become.

Optional glide path

If the money is needed at year twenty rather than beyond it, from year fifteen shift three percentage points a year out of equity into GOLDBEES and LIQUIDBEES — so the outcome isn't hostage to one bad final year.

Risk

What would make this fail

An honest list, because a portfolio document without one is marketing.

  • 01You sell in the drawdown. 75% equity plus 15% metals can fall 40–45% peak to trough. This has happened repeatedly and will happen again inside twenty years. Every projection above assumes you did nothing. If you can't hold that, cut equity to 60% and accept the lower expected return — a portfolio you keep beats a better one you abandon.
  • 02India's twenty-year growth story disappoints. No amount of low-cost structuring saves you from a flat market. This is the irreducible bet, and it is the one you are actually making.
  • 03Metals go nowhere for a decade. Gold did roughly that from 1980 to 2000. The 15% weight is sized so this is survivable rather than fatal.
  • 04Expense ratios drift upward. Recheck at every April review. If NIFTYBEES stops being among the cheapest, move — the loyalty is to the cost, not to the brand.
  • 05You rebalance too often. Every trade is a spread plus a tax event. Bands, not tinkering.
  • 06Concentration you didn't plan for. ₹1 crore should not be everything you have. Keep six to twelve months of expenses and any five-year goal money entirely outside this portfolio — otherwise Rule 05 becomes impossible to follow at exactly the moment it matters.
